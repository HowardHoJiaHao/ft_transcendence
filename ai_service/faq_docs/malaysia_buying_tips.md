# Buying Badminton Gear in Malaysia on RacketDiction

RacketDiction is built around how Malaysians already trade rackets: hall meet-ups, MRT stations, DuitNow, and a healthy fear of clone frames. This page collects local practice. It is guidance, not a guarantee.

## 1. Clone versus genuine

Flagship Yonex, Victor, Li-Ning, and Apacs frames are heavily cloned. A clone sold **as a clone** at a clone price is a different thing from a clone sold as an Astrox 99 Pro.

Ask for photos of:

* The **cone** (shape, paint, font)
* Any **serial** or inner-shaft marking
* The **joint** between shaft and head
* The **grommet** shape and paint around 12 o’clock
* The **cover** if they claim it is original

Then:

* Compare those photos with a trusted shop unit if you can.
* Be suspicious of a “brand new 100ZZ” far below current boutique or studio price.
* Remember that even genuine second-hand frames can have hidden cracks. Authenticity is not the same as condition.

RacketBot will not declare a listing genuine from a title. Listing text is a seller claim.

## 2. Rough RM price sense (used club market)

These are **order-of-magnitude** club-sale bands, not official retail:

* Generic or older aluminium / beginner frames: often well under RM150
* Mid used 4U club rackets in honest condition: commonly a few hundred RM
* Recent flagship used frames: can sit in the RM400–RM800 band depending on wear and model
* Brand-new current flagships from a real shop are usually higher than a used listing — that gap is normal; a **new** listing at one-third of shop price is not

Strings, overgrips, and nylon shuttles should look like consumable prices. If a “new BG80 reel” is impossibly cheap, assume a generic rewind.

Always compare with what you see at a local studio or Decathlon / sports shop that week. Prices move.

## 3. Where to meet

Works well in the Klang Valley and most state capitals:

* The hall you both already book (for example a public arena or university hall)
* MRT / LRT / KTM concourses during busy hours
* Mall atriums next to a padded seating area so you can inspect the frame

Avoid secluded car parks at night and “I will send my cousin to your house.”

Bring the listing photos on your phone so you can match paint chips.

## 4. How people actually pay

* **Cash** at the hall after inspection.
* **DuitNow** to the seller’s own account after inspection. Check the name matches what they told you in chat.
* **Cart Meet up / COD** so the quantity and price are recorded, then still pay as above at handover.
* **Stripe sandbox** on this project is test-mode only and only for the demo seller. It is not the usual way two club players settle a used Astrox.

Do not DuitNow a deposit to “hold” a flagship you have never seen.

## 5. Postage between states

KL ↔ Penang, JB, Ipoh, KK, Kuching is normal.

* Buyer usually pays postage unless the seller is clearing stock.
* Ask for a racket tube or cardboard corner protection. A padded envelope is not enough for a carbon frame.
* Use a courier with tracking (J&T, PosLaju, DHL eCommerce, and similar).
* Photograph the packed item. Seller should send the tracking number the same day.
* Heat in a van will not usually ruin a frame; a fold in the middle of a soft parcel will.

## 6. Language and listings

* Titles mix English, Malay, and Chinese brand names. Search for the model (`88D`, `100ZZ`, `Thruster`) as well as the full words.
* The site UI can be switched to English, Bahasa Melayu, or 中文; the seller’s description stays as they typed it.
* “Near new”, “like new”, and “99% new” still need photos of the grommets.

## 7. Hall-culture extras worth asking

* What tension it is on now, and whether it was strung on a drop-weight or electronic machine
* Whether it has been used for singles only or for box / social doubles (wear pattern)
* Whether the seller will meet at a specific hall so you can hit a few shots before paying (only if both are comfortable)

A few shots will not reveal every crack, but they will reveal a dead shaft or a badly unbalanced DIY paint job.
