# Account Settings, Security and Platform Safety

Your account is how you buy, sell, chat, and talk to RacketBot about your own orders. Treat it like a shop key: if someone else has it, they can message as you and place orders.

This page is about staying safe. For the step-by-step sign-in methods, see the account login guide.

## 1. How accounts are protected

* New password accounts are created with email OTP as a second step at login. After you enter the correct password, the site emails a **6-digit one-time code**.
* Codes expire quickly. Use **Resend OTP** if the email is late; there is a short cooldown.
* Google sign-in uses your Google account instead of a RacketDiction password. The security of that Google account is your responsibility.
* Passwords are stored as hashes, not as readable text.
* Changing email or password from **Profile** also asks for an OTP sent to your current email. Google-managed accounts cannot change email or password here.
* HTTPS and a web application firewall sit in front of the site. That reduces some attacks; it does not make every user honest.

Never share your password, OTP, or API key. RacketBot, sellers, and “support” accounts will not ask for them.

## 2. Profile details that affect safety

* Keep your **email** current so OTP mail can reach you.
* Add a **phone number** if you sell or use meet-up orders; buyers may see a seller phone after checkout so they can arrange handover.
* Use a real **avatar** and username you are willing to be recognised by at a court.
* A **suspended** account cannot use the marketplace normally. Contact the project operator if you believe that was a mistake.

## 3. Recognising scams and clone rackets

Common patterns on Malaysian racket groups also appear here:

* **Too cheap.** A Yonex Astrox 99 Pro, 100ZZ, or similar flagship listed far below street price (for example RM100 when clubs sell it near RM700–RM900) is often a clone or a bait listing.
* **Deposit first.** Anyone who demands a deposit because “another buyer is offering more” before you have seen the racket is a risk.
* **Move off-platform immediately.** Asking you to WhatsApp and pay before any inspection or order is a warning sign, not a convenience.
* **Catalogue-only photos.** Stock images with no cone, no serial area, and no wear marks make it harder to prove the seller has the actual frame.
* **Urgent mailing only.** A seller who refuses any public meet-up and will only accept an instant transfer to a personal account is harder to verify.

Clone rackets exist. Some are sold honestly as replicas; selling them as genuine is not allowed.

## 4. Safer buying habits

* Prefer public meet-ups at a badminton hall, MRT/LRT station, or mall.
* Inspect the frame, shaft, grommets, and shake-test the handle before you pay cash or DuitNow.
* For mailing, check the listing, chat history, and reviews first. Ask for a tracking number the same day it is posted.
* Use the in-app cart when you want the purchase recorded. Meetup cash outside the cart is entirely between you and the seller.
* Stripe checkout on this project is **sandbox / test mode**. It does not charge real money and is limited to the configured demo seller.

## 5. Safer selling habits

* Do not accept “I already transferred, release the item” screenshots as proof.
* Meet in public. Bring a friend if the item is expensive.
* Count cash or confirm DuitNow in your own banking app before handing over the racket.
* Keep chat on RacketDiction so you have a record if something goes wrong.

## 6. Reporting a problem

* If someone is selling clones as genuine, harassing you in chat, or trying to phish your login, keep screenshots of the listing, profile, chat, and any payment evidence.
* Contact the project operator through the **Contact** link in the footer and attach that evidence.
* RacketDiction does not currently expose a complete in-app report workflow. Do not rely on a Report button being available.
* Staff with a moderator or admin role can suspend accounts that break the rules. That is a staff action, not something RacketBot can do.

## 7. What the platform does not guarantee

* It does not guarantee that a listing is genuine, that a seller will appear, or that a mailed item will arrive undamaged.
* It is not a party to the sale and is not obliged to mediate or issue refunds.
* Chat, reviews, and OTP login reduce some risks. They do not remove them.
